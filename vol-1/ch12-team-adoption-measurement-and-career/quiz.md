# Chapter 12 quiz

1. Assisted drafts reach review sooner, but total review effort rises. Which
   clocks and outcomes would you bring to the decision meeting?
2. A maintainer abandons a task after an hour of prompting and review. Where
   does that hour go, and what is the denominator for acceptance?
3. Four changes were accepted. Three have finished follow-up and none of
   those escaped review with a defect. How do you report observed quality?
4. When can a seat bill and a token bill be added without counting the same
   usage twice?
5. Which onboarding assignment gives a junior developer practice reviewing
   an agent's work before relying on it in production?

## Answers

1. Bring elapsed time, active author and reviewer time, acceptance and observed
   quality. Read them alongside reviewer capacity and any post-release work.
2. Assign active minutes to the relevant roles and keep the task and its spend
   in its original condition. Acceptance uses all assigned tasks.
3. Record zero escaped defects out of three observations and one accepted
   change awaiting follow-up. The pending change has an unknown outcome.
4. When the metered spend is separately billed, such as an account outside
   the seats or usage beyond the seats' included allowance. Reconcile the
   buckets and dates against the invoices.
5. Have them predict a rule result, trace the path and write a failing check.
   Then let the agent propose a patch. Ask them to explain the diff and use
   their check to verify the result.
